    ELECTROMAGNETIC WAVES DURING DISASTERS

    Electromagnetic waves are invisible forms of energy that travel through space at the speed of light. They play a vital role in communication, detection, rescue operations, and saving lives during disasters.

    ROLES THAT MATTER

  • Invisible Energy: Electromagnetic waves are invisible energy waves that travel through space at the speed of light (\approx 300,000\text{ km/s}).
  • Information Delivery: They carry signals that help us send and receive messages, pictures, data, and sounds.
  • Critical in Disasters: Used in communication, warnings, locating survivors, and sharing important information.
  • Connect. Inform. Save.: These waves connect people and provide help when it matters most.

    • REAL-WORLD USES DURING DISASTERS

  • Emergency Radio: Provides important updates, alerts, and instructions during emergencies.
  • Satellite Communication: Enables long-distance communication and helps with GPS, weather updates, and rescue coordination.
  • Rescue Team Communication: Helps rescue teams stay connected and share information in the field.
  • Wireless Networks: Cell towers, radios, and microwaves keep signals moving even in crisis situations.
  • Thermal Imaging (Infrared): Detects heat from survivors, even in darkness, smoke, or debris.

    • LIGHT BEHAVIOR

    Reflection of Light

  • Definition: Light bounces back when it hits a smooth surface.
  • Diagram Elements: Incident light, Normal line, Reflected light.
  • Example: A flashlight beam reflecting on a mirror.

    • Refraction of Light

  • Definition: Light changes direction when it passes from one medium to another.
  • Diagram Elements: Air, Water.
  • Example: A pencil appearing bent in a glass of water.

    THE ELECTROMAGNETIC SPECTRUM

    (Longer Wavelength / Lower Energy \longleftrightarrow Shorter Wavelength / Higher Energy)

    | Wave Type | Wavelength Range | Application |

    | Radio Waves | > 1\text{ m} | Used for emergency broadcasts and communication. |

    | Microwaves | 1\text{ m} - 1\text{ mm} | Used in satellite communication and weather monitoring. |

    | Infrared | 1\text{ mm} - 700\text{ nm} | Used in thermal imaging to detect heat from people and objects. |

    | Visible Light | 700\text{ nm} - 400\text{ nm} | Helps us see. Used in flashlights and rescue operations. |

    | Ultraviolet | 400\text{ nm} - 10\text{ nm} | Helps in sterilization and detecting certain hazards. |

    | X-Rays | 10\text{ nm} - 0.01\text{ nm} | Used to scan objects and help in medical emergencies. |

    | Gamma Rays | 0.01\text{ nm} | Used in space observations and cancer treatment. |

    WHY IT'S IMPORTANT DURING DISASTERS

  • Sends early warnings for typhoons, earthquakes, tsunamis, and other hazards.
  • Helps rescuers communicate and coordinate in affected areas.
  • Locates and monitors survivors using sensors and devices.
  • Provides information through radio, TV, and the internet.
  • Keeps communities connected and informed, reducing panic and saving lives.
  • Supports quick decisions and helps people receive the help they need.
